01 The law Can a landlord in Italy keep my deposit? 1590 the article that answers

Only for documented damage.
Not for silence.

Under art. 1590 of the civil code the deposit goes back at the end of the lease. Deductions are allowed for damage beyond normal wear or unpaid rent — itemised and proven. “There were damages” with no list, no photos and no invoice is not a legal ground; it is a negotiating position.

Art. 1590 c.c.

What the law actually says

  • The deposit must be returned at the end of the lease
  • Deductions only for documented damage beyond normal wear, or unpaid rent
  • Itemised and proven — silence and vague claims are not legal grounds
What they usually say

The three lines we hear every week

  • “There were damages” — with no list and no photos
  • “It covers the cleaning” — with no invoice
  • Nothing at all: they stop answering
What decides it

Paper beats memory

  • The handover record with meter readings and photos
  • The contract clause on how the deposit comes back
  • A formal demand citing the right article, sent properly

06 The counterclaim The landlord says I damaged the flat. 1 document decides it

Then it has to be
proven, not stated.

Damage beyond normal wear must be itemised, documented and costed — photos, an invoice, a comparison with the state of the flat when you moved in. That is exactly why the handover record matters: with a signed one, this argument ends quickly. Without it, it becomes your word against theirs — and we work with what exists.

R01 Territorial agreement

Rome's negotiated-rent bands

The 2023 Rome agreement fixes €/m² bands per zone. The wrong rent can't be attested — you pay more and lose the tax break.

We compute the attestable band for your flat — 75 zones, the official coefficients — and negotiate against it.

R02 Italian law

Deposit: three months, maximum

Anything above is not enforceable — and “non-refundable deposit” is not a thing.

We push for two, in writing, with the return terms named in the contract.

R03 Landlord's duty

Registered within 30 days — or it doesn't exist

An unregistered lease gives you no legal protection and no residency.

We check the registration setup before you sign and follow the protocol number after.

R04 €5 · public registry

Whoever rents it must own it

The fake-landlord scam dies with one document: the visura catastale names the real owner.

We pull it before a single euro moves.

R05 L. 431/98 art. 5

A transitional lease needs a documented reason

The wrong contract type for your stay is the most common “legal” trap for foreigners.

We pick the right form — transitional, student, 4+4 — and check the attestation that makes it hold.

R06 Handover

The keys day is a document, not a handshake

No readings, no photos, no signed state of the flat — and the deposit argument is your word against theirs.

We make sure the contract requires a written handover record.
