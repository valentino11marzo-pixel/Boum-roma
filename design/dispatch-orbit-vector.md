# Dispatch, Orbit and Vector

7 November2026. Surface mode: Persuade, followed by the existing Operate flows.
Valentino identifies Dispatch's style and structure and Orbit's immersive
impression as the directions to retain. He asks for both to be refined throughout
the experience and for a third option combining them. This is a precise extension
of two accepted worlds, not a new identity exercise or an open concept tournament.
The original Helvetica Neue300, BOOM mark, black/yellow/white, Solari, geographic
canvas, actual homes and native service journeys remain the authority.

## The three compositions

Dispatch keeps its large six-city directory and white contextual plate. On a
phone the same six controls form a two-column directory so the selected city and
photographs arrive earlier. Its structural language now extends into filters,
home review, actual-cost information and the foreign-city brief. The real service
routes and fee stubs remain; the inherited metallic strip is removed.

Orbit keeps its centered welcome and oversized geographic horizon. Solari belongs
to the welcome instead of floating away from it. The phone city rail and shorter
opening reveal the globe sooner. A precise open planner, restrained white service
tickets and the same light display hierarchy continue through catalogue, home
detail, desks, comparison and footer. Filtered pairs use their available width.

Vector combines the destination directory with that original horizon. A narrower
left directory selects the destination; a white context plate provides the next
action above the black geographic scene. Rome also exposes its existing home
photographs. The actual planner spans both columns below. On phones the directory
becomes three columns and two rows, followed by context, scene and the same form.
Lower sections reuse Dispatch's visual rules rather than creating a third copy of
the customer journey. The original globe renderer owns horizon/full-view geometry.

Studio foregrounds Dispatch, Orbit and Vector, with Dispatch as the default.
The other four directions and the historical versions remain accessible in the
secondary comparison controls. Valid public preferences survive switching.

## Craft and behavior

| Before | After | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tablet Dispatch promise fell to15px | Minimum16px | The body-text floor must hold between phone and desktop |
| Six phone directory rows delayed the selected context | The same buttons in two columns in Dispatch; three in Vector | Retain each composition while reducing the initial distance to the offer |
| Heavy archived display rules reappeared in foreign desks and detail | Explicit300 hierarchy in the refined scopes | Identity must continue after the first screen |
| Cost/brief surfaces inherited unrelated controls and colours | Shared white decision surfaces, readable fields, yellow action and appropriate focus/error colours | Important decisions should remain recognisable and legible |
| Desktop Locate could select a map outside the viewport | The original handler also reveals the map in the three refined directions | Selection needs a visible consequence |
| Scene and support buttons had pressed state alone | Controls name their actual panels through aria-controls | Expose the relation without adding duplicate state or forms |
| Vector inherited a three-column action and tiny phone departure record | A two-column action with its arrow at the edge, plus an18px Solari row | Make the next action and long destination names legible |
| The inherited phone cost grid squeezed fees and split the rent unit | A single reading column in all three refined directions | Read rent, availability, full costs, action and next steps in order |

No additional animation loop, dependency, form, backend, customer store or
commercial claim is introduced. The original globe, Solari, photo rail, native
scroll, pause and reduced-motion behavior continue. Keyboard scene changes stay
immediate; pointer scene changes retain the existing finite transition. Original
service fees and conditions remain visible, with no automatic purchase or send.

Interaction references: [WAI button pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/button/)
for native action and stable pressed-state labels;
[MDN aria-controls](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Accessibility/ARIA/Reference/Attributes/aria-controls)
for the relationship to controlled panels; and
[MDN reduced motion](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@media/prefers-reduced-motion)
for the existing visitor preference. These inform semantics, not claims of
conversion uplift. No new animation or performance result is inferred from them.
