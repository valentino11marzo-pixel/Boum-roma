## Structural exploration

Seven structures considered, in order of resonance: Cityside (the everyday
destination and city map), Folio (a continuous photographic home story), Gate
(the help needed now), Dispatch (a large typographic destination directory),
Homebook (the actual collection as the opening), Arrivals (the existing journey
as a readable sequence), Connection (the global and local paths in one scene).
Impeccable surface seed:5c54185d5dbcb1e0 dealt4,3,7: Dispatch, Gate, Connection.
These become functional alternatives for review, with no imposed selection.

Dispatch makes the six real destinations the visual and interactive structure.
Its black directory uses large300 names; a white contextual plate shows the
selected city's real home imagery or geography. This changes the navigation
hierarchy, not merely its colour. Its risk is pushing the offer too low on a
phone; compact selected-city context and a direct catalogue action must remain.

Gate starts with the visitor's situation and the real support products. The
same service section moves into the opening, with scope, fee and native action
as the primary content. It is useful for someone who has found a home or has a
contract already. Rome catalogue access stays obvious; foreign visitors still
receive their original desk offer. Its risk is mistaking assistance for the only
BOOM product; the home route remains visible above the service decision.

Connection lets the actual Rome Skyline lead the scene, with photographs and
global geography available as alternate scales. The original everyday-place
chooser becomes an opening action. The existing rent/space/modelled-journey
comparison precedes the results after a committed native request. A cobalt
cartographic surface is an exploratory secondary colour alongside the original
yellow mark/actions, black map and300 display voice. Its risk is map loading or
complexity: original loading/failure exits, clear scene controls and ordinary
catalogue links are essential. No fake map or new geographic engine is added.

## Grounding and boundaries

The original single Skyline iframe belongs to BoomNextMap. Connection moves its
container, never its internals or customer state. Only a visible home city scene
mounts the home map; other home scenes hide it, while Apartments and Detail keep
their native lifecycle. Native location actions reveal the city scene first.
Foreign cities never show Rome as their own inventory. A private destination
remains private; unsubmitted form edits never claim recalculated comparisons.

The original #nextBalance container can be moved before #nextDiscovery without
retaining generated cards. The original destination guide and filters own all
transitions, modelled-journey explanations and privacy boundaries. Gate moves
the four original tickets and chooser as one section, preserving terms/fees.
No new form, lead, backend, dependency, telemetry or invented proof is added.

Primary references inform composition, not claims of affiliation or uplift:
- [Airbus A350](https://www.aircraft.airbus.com/en/aircraft/a350): continuity
  between planning and geographic context, translated into the existing journey.
- [FAA electronic flight strips](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/technology/tfdm/efs):
  a compact record stays useful through successive decisions and handoffs.
- [Lufthansa digital companion](https://www.lufthansa.com/de/de/book-and-manage/digital-self-services/digital-travel-companion):
  assistance organised around the traveller's current stage.

The external surface challengers are not substituted for BOOM's pinned visual
identity: live-code music, bitmap teletext, glazed pottery, surreal clouds and
misregistered zines would introduce unrelated control metaphors or typography.
Their useful discipline is a clear primary act and coherent state vocabulary;
the actual BOOM forms, geography and service conditions supply those here.
