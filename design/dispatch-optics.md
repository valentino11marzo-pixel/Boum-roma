# Dispatch: optics, contact and a continuous reading path

7 November 2026. Refinement of the accepted dark Dispatch entrance. The user
asked for another engineered variation and greater care in scrolling, buttons,
selection, motion and 3D. The product, original Helvetica Neue300, black/yellow
identity, Solari, white decision ticket and white company footer remain.

## Motion thesis

The authored moment is an optical change of the same geographic object:
Globe shows the complete sphere; Horizon brings the foreground closer. The
two lenses share the actual Natural Earth canvas, city, orientation and zoom.
They change neither the visitor's brief nor the service. Default Horizon is
retained. Full view temporarily shows the complete globe and restores the lens
on close. Lens controls are absent inside that full view, where they would
describe an unavailable framing.

| Element | Role | Starts | Duration | Relationship |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Globe geometry | Lead | Pointer choice | 320ms | Radius and centre change together on the original renderer clock |
| Selected lens | State | Choice | Immediate | Labels stay still; yellow identifies the requested perspective |
| Primary control | Feedback | Press | 140ms | The complete control compresses subtly and releases from its current state |
| Chapter marker | Orientation | Actual section crossing | 220ms | Existing aria-current owns the mark; it is not purchase progress |

Repeated lens choices reverse from the current frame. Keyboard, pause, reduced
motion, hidden page and offscreen states settle the optical movement. The
renderer has no second animation loop, shader library or downloaded asset.
Physical-device performance is not established by desktop browser tests.

Reading material does not travel: prices, scope, founder identity, form labels
and the service ticket remain stable. No delayed section reveals hide content.
The existing Solari remains the only animated destination lettering.

## Contact and scrolling

Primary actions use the original yellow with dark text. Utility controls remain
quiet; a selected city, need or lens uses yellow. Held pressure, release, focus
and disabled states have explicit treatments. Fine-pointer hover does not
become a persistent touch state. Pausing motion also removes spatial press
feedback. Focus remains visible inside joined/clipped control groups.

The original journey navigation becomes a compact sticky locator. It reads the
same original current-section controller, with a threshold at the actual header
and route bars. Their measured heights replace conflicting fixed anchor
offsets for Dispatch. Wheel, touch momentum, scrollbar and browser anchors
retain native behavior; there is no virtual scroll engine or compulsory route.

## Technical references and limits

The web platform guidance supports preserving native scrolling and progressive
enhancement; a scroll-driven timeline was considered but adds no needed meaning
to this interface, whose section ownership already exists. See [Chrome's
scrollytelling guidance](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/modern-web-guidance/guides/ui-behaviors/scrollytelling)
and [WebKit's scroll-driven animation explanation](https://webkit.org/blog/17101/a-guide-to-scroll-driven-animations-with-just-css/).
The installed Impeccable, animate, choreograph and Emil design-engineering
guidance informed the interruption, timing and feedback decisions.

This work remains the separate Preview entrance. No new analytics, lead store,
payment logic, public-home migration or conversion claim is introduced.
