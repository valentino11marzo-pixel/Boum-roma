# Dispatch: the departure hall

7 November 2026. A change of composition after the user found the optical
refinement too limited. This supersedes the compact directory and shared
photo/world opening described in `dispatch-departure.md` and `dispatch-optics.md`.

## Composition and hierarchy

The original Solari and a single primary action occupy the left of a broad
departure stage. The real geographic canvas occupies the right, with enough
height for its Horizon perspective. Six city choices form a continuous rail
below; the filled native brief and planner follow. On phones the reading order
is destination, geography, city choices and plan. No destination is hidden in a
carousel, and nothing waits for an entrance animation to become available.

Geography now owns Dispatch's opening even in Rome. The same original photo
gallery moves into the Rome homes chapter before the collection. It retains
its catalogue, handlers and image identity. Full view continues to move and
restore the same atlas using its original anchor. Other visions retain their
scene choices. The authored finite lens/city motion and its original clock are
unchanged; native scrolling and reduced motion remain.

The European service area is a compact chooser beside one white ticket. The
actual offer, price, conditions, refund, inclusions and original brief belong to
that ticket. Rome's four real services become two columns of paper boarding
passes, with the real service colours retained at their edges. Prices and
conditions sit on the same surface as their action, separated by a perforation.
Mobile uses one column. White remains the decision and company surface, black
the destination surface, and yellow the action/selection colour. The original
Helvetica Neue300 and Solari lettering are retained.

## Implementation boundaries

New CSS files own composition only. `boom-flight-visions.js` moves original
nodes; `boom-dispatch-journey.js` places its read-only summary after the city
rail. No new form, customer state, API, dependency, price calculation, payment
logic, public-home migration or tracking is introduced.

## Validation

Registered browser suites cover original forms and node identity, city and
service choices, native date/currency validation, privacy, Full view, motion
interruption and the other six visions. Rendered review uses the actual public
catalogue and checks the complete Rome and European compositions at multiple
viewport widths. Browser automation blocks form/payment writes. Conversion
uplift and performance on physical phones are not established by these checks.

The final measured canvas is 771×440px at a1440px viewport, 392×360px at768px,
350×256px at390px and 280×256px at320px. The phone console keeps four44px-high
controls on one row; attribution shares the lens row. Rome and foreign cities
retain the same stage height. The rail and actual Solari flaps fit Amsterdam.

The independent rendered review found no remaining material design issue after
left-aligning the supporting copy, moving the CTA arrow to its terminal edge
and correcting the inherited phone grid. European error text now uses dark red
on the white ticket. Shared-HTML static detection also traversed legacy linked
styles and raised contrast/decorative notices; no clean whole-site audit is
claimed. Screenshots and scoped browser assertions are the evidence for this
Dispatch composition.

Final registered checks: Dispatch17/17, Panorama44/44 and Club motion8/8 pass
on the final source. An earlier combined run timed out in Orbit1440 and the
