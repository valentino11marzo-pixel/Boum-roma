How to send it — this part matters

  1. Print and sign the letter. Add the place and date by hand.
  2. Send it by raccomandata A/R (registered mail with return receipt) or PEC (certified email). An ordinary email creates no proof.
  3. Keep the receipt. It is what proves your landlord was formally put in default — the whole point of the exercise.
  4. Give them the 15 days. Most cases settle inside 2–6 weeks of a proper letter.

Why Italian, if I don't speak it?

Because your landlord does. The letter cites art. 1590 of the Italian Civil Code — normal wear is not damage — and puts them formally in default under artt. 1219 and 2943. That language is what changes the tone of the conversation.

FAQ

Good to know.

Is this really free?
Yes, no catch. Most people who lose a deposit in Italy lose it to distance and language, not to law — and a proper letter fixes both. If your landlord ignores it, that's when our paid service exists.
Can a landlord in Italy keep my deposit?
Only for damages beyond normal wear, itemized and proven, or unpaid rent. Under art. 1590 c.c. normal use is not damage, and vague claims or silence are not legal grounds. The burden of proving the damage is on the landlord, not on you.
I already left Italy — does this still work?
Yes. The letter carries your address abroad, and PEC or registered mail can be sent from anywhere (an Italian friend or a service can post it for you). Our paid recovery works fully remotely too.
What if I don't have the check-in photos?
You can still send the letter. Without a check-out report the landlord has to prove the damage, which is harder than most assume — that's why formal letters work even when your documentation is thin.
Is this legal advice?
No. It's a well-built template following the standard Italian formal-notice structure. BOOM is a licensed rental agency, not a law firm — and the document itself says so.
