IN BRIEF

The 60-second version. Save it.

Rome rental safety, in six facts
  • Never send money for an apartment you haven't entered — not a deposit, not a "reservation", not a "good-faith payment". A landlord who is "abroad" and will "ship the keys" is a scam, every time.
  • Ownership is checkable for €5. A visura catastale (property registry extract) shows who really owns the apartment. The name on it must match the ID of the person signing.
  • Only a registered contract protects you. It must be filed with the Agenzia delle Entrate within 30 days and you must receive the copy with a protocol number. Unregistered = no rights.
  • Pay only by traceable bank transfer or card — never cash, never Western Union, never crypto. The paper trail is your legal protection.
  • A price far below market is bait. One-bedrooms in central Rome ask €1,200–1,800 in 2026; a "€800 gem in Trastevere" is fishing for your deposit.
  • Urgency is the weapon. "Decide within hours or it's gone" is how every scam closes. A real landlord can wait for a video call and a document check.
THE FIVE RULES

What stops 95% of scams, mechanically.

1

See it live before any money moves

Enter the apartment yourself, or have someone verify it on a live video call — walking the actual rooms, not playing a pre-recorded clip. Photos prove nothing: phantom listings scrape them from real ads. Can't be in Rome? A Virtual Viewing (€89) sends us to walk any listing live, with an honest report and the red flags included.

Never: wiring a deposit sight-unseen
2

Verify the owner with the €5 document

Ask for a visura catastale — the official property registry extract. It costs about €5, takes minutes, and shows who owns the property. Match that name to the ID of the person signing. Subletters posing as owners and "tenants on the way out" pocketing deposits both die on this one document. If they refuse to provide it, they don't own it.

Never: taking an ID alone as proof of ownership
3

Registered contract, or no contract

An Italian lease must be registered with the Agenzia delle Entrate within 30 days of signing, and you must receive the registered copy with its protocol number. Without it the contract barely exists legally: no eviction protection, no deposit protection. Both parties' codice fiscale must appear, and the deposit can never exceed three months by law. Unsure what you're reading? Our contract types guide explains transitorio vs 4+4 — or the Contract Check Express (€49) gives you a written verdict in 24 hours.

Never: "we'll register it later, don't worry"
4

Traceable money only

Deposit and rent travel by bank transfer (bonifico) or card — with the address and month in the payment description. Cash "to keep things simple" means no proof you ever paid, and usually a landlord evading taxes with your name attached. Collecting deposits from several candidates at once (the "double deposit") only works when the money is untraceable.

Never: cash, Western Union, gift cards, crypto
5

Document the handover

On key day, do a walkthrough and sign a verbale di consegna — a handover report with meter readings and dated photos of every room. It's the document that gets your deposit back at the end (under art. 1590 of the Civil Code, normal wear is not damage). Your rights, clause by clause: the tenant rights cheat sheet.

Never: accepting keys with nothing in writing

FAQ

Asked before every deposit.

Is it safe to rent an apartment in Rome online?
Yes — if the verification happens before the money. A live video viewing of the actual rooms, the €5 visura catastale matched to the signer's ID, a contract registered with the Agenzia delle Entrate and a traceable payment make a remote rental safer than most in-person handshakes. Skip any of the four and you're gambling.
How do I verify who really owns an apartment in Italy?
Request a visura catastale — the official property registry extract. It costs around €5 and names the legal owner. Match that name against the ID of the person signing your contract. If they won't provide it, walk away: subletters posing as owners are one of Rome's most expensive scams.
What is the most common rental scam in Rome?
The phantom listing: a beautiful apartment at a below-market price, a "landlord abroad" who can't show it, and a request to wire the deposit in exchange for shipped keys. The apartment doesn't exist or isn't theirs. Never send money for a home you haven't entered or had verified live.
Can I get my money back after a rental scam?
Wired money is very hard to recover — report it to the Polizia Postale immediately and ask your bank about recall or chargeback if you paid by card. Prevention is worth a hundred recoveries. A deposit withheld by a real landlord is a different, winnable case: start with our free formal demand letter under art. 1590.
